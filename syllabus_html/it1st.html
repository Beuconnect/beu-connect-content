Lab Subjects

Engineering Mathematics - I

  • Unit- 1.0: Basic Linear Algebra (8 hrs)
    • 1.1 Elementary transformations and Rank of Matrix; Rank by Echelon and Normal form; linear dependence & independence of vectors in Rⁿ space
    • 1.2 Consistency of System of Linear Equations
    • 1.3 Eigenvalues & Eigenvectors; Cayley-Hamilton Theorem
    • 1.4 Similarity of Matrices & Diagonalization of Square Matrices; Quadratic Form; Canonical Form of Matrices
  • Unit- 2.0: Differential Calculus: Single Variable (8 hrs)
    • 2.1 Higher order derivatives; Successive differentiation and Leibnitz’s Theorem
    • 2.2 Mean Value Theorems (Rolle’s; Lagrange’s; Cauchy)
    • 2.3 Indeterminate Forms; L’Hopital Rule
    • 2.4 Tangents & Normal of Algebraic & Polar Curves; Curvature
  • Unit- 3.0: Differential Calculus: Several Variables (9 hrs)
    • 3.1 Partial differentiations; Euler’s theorems
    • 3.2 Limit, Continuity and differentiability of functions of Several Variables
    • 3.3 Taylor’s theorem with various forms of remainders; Maclaurin’s theorem and related problems
    • 3.4 Maxima & Minima for functions of several variables; Lagrange’s Multiplier Method
  • Unit- 4.0: Integral Calculus (9 hrs)
    • 4.1 Differentiation under the integral sign; Leibnitz’s rule
    • 4.2 Double integral; Change the order of integration; evaluation by changing to polar co-ordinates
    • 4.3 Triple Integral; evaluation by changing to Spherical Polar and Cylindrical polar co-ordinates
    • 4.4 Beta; Gamma; Elliptic integral; relation between Beta & Gamma functions; general properties and related problems
  • Unit- 5.0: Vector Calculus (8 hrs)
    • 5.1 Scalar and vector valued functions; gradient, divergence and curl; geometrical and physical significance; tangent plane; directional derivative
    • 5.2 Line, surface and volume integrals; Green’s, Stokes’ and Gauss divergence theorems; verification and evaluation of vector integrals

Engineering Physics

  • Unit- 1.0: Wave Optics (8 hrs)
    • 1.1 Interference, division of amplitude, Newton’s Ring experiment, Michelson interferometer
    • 1.2 Diffraction, Fraunhofer diffraction, single and double slit, circular aperture
    • 1.3 Diffraction grating
    • 1.4 Rayleigh criterion; resolving power of telescope
    • 1.5 Polarization, double-refracting crystal, Nicol prism
  • Unit- 2.0: Lasers and Optical Fiber (8 hrs)
    • 2.1 Characteristics of laser light; Einstein’s A & B coefficients; population inversion; pumping mechanism; optical resonator
    • 2.2 Ruby laser, He-Ne laser and semiconductor laser
    • 2.3 Structure of optical fibre; TIR, acceptance angle and numerical aperture; types of optical fibres
    • 2.4 Propagation mechanism of light in fibre; attenuation in optical fibres
    • 2.5 Applications of optical fibres; fibre optic sensors
  • Unit- 3.0: Electromagnetism & Propagation of Waves (8 hrs)
    • 3.1 Electric fields, Gauss’ Law, dielectrics and capacitors
    • 3.2 Magnetic fields and magnetic materials
    • 3.3 Maxwell’s Equations
    • 3.4 Electromagnetic waves and Poynting Theorem
  • Unit- 4.0: Quantum Mechanics (8 hrs)
    • 4.1 Introduction to Quantum Mechanics
    • 4.2 De-Broglie hypothesis; Heisenberg uncertainty principle
    • 4.3 Wave function and its characteristics
    • 4.4 Schrödinger’s equation (time-dependent and independent)
    • 4.5 Particle in a box; energy eigenvalues and eigenfunctions
    • 4.6 Potential barrier and tunneling
  • Unit- 5.0: Semiconductors & Nano-materials (10 hrs)
    • 5.1 Introduction; types of materials (metal, semiconductor and insulator)
    • 5.2 Intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductors; P-type and N-type; diffusion, drift and Fermi level
    • 5.3 Photodiode, P-N junction transistor, LED
    • 5.4 Hall effect
    • 5.5 Solar cell and its characteristics
    • 5.6 Introduction to nanoscience & nanotechnology; significance of nanoscale
    • 5.7 Unique properties: bulk vs nanomaterials; surface-to-volume ratio; band gap and optical/electrical/mechanical properties
    • 5.8 Classification of nanostructured materials: 0D (quantum dot), 1D (nanowire)

Introduction to AI

  • Unit- 1.0: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence (8 hrs)
    • 1.1 What is AI? History and evolution of AI
    • 1.2 Foundations of AI: philosophy, mathematics and cognitive-science influences
    • 1.3 Intelligent agents: agent–environment interaction; PEAS description
    • 1.4 Types of environments: observable, deterministic, episodic, static, discrete, single/multi-agent
    • 1.5 Types of agents: simple reflex, model-based, goal-based, utility-based, learning agents
    • 1.6 Overview of AI application domains across engineering disciplines
  • Unit- 2.0: Problem Solving by Search (9 hrs)
    • 2.1 Problem formulation and state-space representation
    • 2.2 Uninformed search: BFS, DFS, Uniform Cost Search, Depth-Limited and Iterative Deepening Search
    • 2.3 Informed (heuristic) search: Greedy Best-First Search, A* algorithm
    • 2.4 Local search and optimization; hill climbing and related concepts
    • 2.5 Adversarial search: game playing, minimax and alpha-beta pruning
  • Unit- 3.0: Knowledge Representation and Logical Reasoning (9 hrs)
    • 3.1 Knowledge representation and its role in AI
    • 3.2 Propositional logic: syntax, semantics, inference and truth tables
    • 3.3 Predicate logic: predicates, quantifiers and representation of facts
    • 3.4 Logical inference and basic rule-based reasoning
    • 3.5 Knowledge-based systems and reasoning under representation constraints
  • Unit- 4.0: Reasoning under Uncertainty (9 hrs)
    • 4.1 Uncertainty in AI and probabilistic reasoning
    • 4.2 Conditional probability and Bayes’ theorem
    • 4.3 Bayesian reasoning and simple probabilistic inference
    • 4.4 Fuzzy sets, membership functions and basic fuzzy logic
    • 4.5 Applications of probabilistic and fuzzy reasoning
  • Unit- 5.0: Introduction to Machine Learning and AI Applications (10 hrs)
    • 5.1 Machine learning paradigms: supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement learning
    • 5.2 Overview of regression and classification: linear regression, k-NN, decision trees
    • 5.3 Overview of clustering: k-means
    • 5.4 Introduction to artificial neural networks: perceptron and basic architecture
    • 5.5 Natural Language Processing (NLP): concept and applications
    • 5.6 Computer Vision: concept and applications
    • 5.7 Expert systems: architecture and applications
    • 5.8 Robotics and AI: overview of intelligent robotic systems
    • 5.9 Ethics, bias, responsible AI, societal impact and AI & Sustainable Development Goals

    • 4.8 Internet and IP Address
    • 4.9 TCP, UDP, HTTP, FTP, DNS, URL and Client-Server Architecture
  • Unit- 5.0: Emerging Technologies (8 hrs)
    • 5.1 Internet of Things (IoT): concept and architecture; Smart Homes and Smart Cities

Basics of Electrical & Electronics Engineering

  • Unit- 1.0: Basic Electrical Circuits (8 hrs)
    • 1.1 Electrical circuit elements: resistance, inductance and capacitance as active and passive elements
    • 1.2 Unilateral and bilateral elements; linear and nonlinear elements
    • 1.3 Ohm’s law and Kirchhoff’s current and voltage laws
    • 1.4 Series and parallel circuits; voltage/current sources; source transformation
    • 1.5 Mesh analysis and nodal analysis
    • 1.6 Current divider rule, voltage divider rule and star-delta transformation
  • Unit- 2.0: A.C. Circuits (8 hrs)
    • 2.1 Common electrical signals and waveform characteristics
    • 2.2 Instantaneous, peak, average and RMS values
    • 2.3 Peak factor and form factor of sinusoidal waves
    • 2.4 Phase difference and phasor representation in rectangular and polar forms
    • 2.5 Impedance, admittance, power factor and power triangle
    • 2.6 Three-phase systems: necessity, advantages, phase sequence and balanced/unbalanced supply and load
  • Unit- 3.0: Basic Electrical Machines (8 hrs)
    • 3.1 Basic laws of electromagnetism, self-inductance and mutual inductance
    • 3.2 DC machines: principle, construction, classification and applications
    • 3.3 Single-phase transformers: necessity, working principle, construction and E.M.F. equation
    • 3.4 Transformer voltage ratio, current ratio and voltage regulation
    • 3.5 Single-phase and three-phase induction motors and working principles
    • 3.6 Applications of induction motors and fractional horsepower motors
  • Unit- 4.0: Semiconductor Devices (8 hrs)
    • 4.1 Intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductor types
    • 4.2 P-N junction diode: construction, characteristics, forward and reverse bias
    • 4.3 Temperature effects and breakdown mechanisms
    • 4.4 Half-wave/full-wave rectifiers, filters and Zener diode voltage regulator
    • 4.5 PNP and NPN transistors: construction and operation
    • 4.6 CE configuration as amplifier and transistor as a switch
  • Unit- 5.0: Fundamentals of Digital, Analog Electronics, Measurement and Electrical Safety (10 hrs)
    • 5.1 Binary, octal and hexadecimal number systems; binary addition and basic number operations
    • 5.2 Basic logic gates, universal gates and Boolean algebra
    • 5.3 LEDs, seven-segment display and truth table; digital and analog ICs
    • 5.4 IC 7805 and IC 7912; multimeter and CRO
    • 5.5 Sensors and transducers; generation, transmission and distribution of electrical power
    • 5.6 Electrical hazards; fuse, MCB and MCCB
    • 5.7 Purpose, importance and methods of earthing systems
