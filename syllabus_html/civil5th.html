Structural Design-I

101501 · 3 Credits
Unit 1.0 — Fundamentals of RC Design and the Working Stress Method 6 hrs

This unit introduces the basic material properties of concrete and steel, and the foundational design concepts using elastic theory. It covers the traditional working stress method (WSM) and its application to different types of beam sections.

  • Concepts of Reinforced Concrete: introduction to RC, stress-strain characteristics of concrete and steel, and elastic theory.
  • Working Stress Method: design philosophy and assumptions of the method.
  • Singly Reinforced Beam Analysis and Design: analysis and design of balanced, under-reinforced, and over-reinforced rectangular sections using WSM.
Unit 2.0 — Limit State Method: Principles and Design of Beams 9 hrs

This unit transitions to the modern limit state method (LSM) of design and applies its concepts to beams for flexure, shear and torsion.

  • Concepts of LSM: principles of probability and reliability, characteristic loads and strengths, partial safety factors for loads and materials, and limit states of collapse and serviceability.
  • Design of beams for flexure: singly reinforced, doubly reinforced, rectangular and T-sections.
  • Design for shear and bond: shear resistance, shear reinforcement, bond and anchorage.
  • Design for bending and torsion: beams subjected to combined bending and torsion as per IS Code recommendations.
Unit 3.0 — Design of Compression Members (Columns) 7 hrs

Design of columns supporting axial and bending loads, including short and slender columns.

  • Design of short columns subjected to direct compression.
  • Design of columns subjected to combined axial load and uni-axial bending moment as per IS Code methods.
  • Design of columns for axial load and bi-axial bending moment.
Unit 4.0 — Design of Slabs and Staircases 6 hrs

Design of common slab systems and different types of staircases.

  • Design of one-way and two-way slabs.
  • Design considerations for flat slabs.
  • Design of dog-legged and open-well type staircases.
Unit 5.0 — Design of Foundations 6 hrs

Design of concrete foundations for transferring building loads to supporting soil.

  • Design of square and rectangular isolated footings subjected to axial load and moments.
  • Design of combined footings for supporting multiple columns.
Unit 6.0 — Prestressed Concrete 8 hrs
  • Principles of prestressed concrete: basic principles, materials, and methods of prestressing (pre-tensioning and post-tensioning).
  • Losses in prestress: elastic shortening, creep, shrinkage and friction.
  • Analysis of prestressed sections: rectangular, T-section, box section and girders subjected to prestress and external loads.
  • Design of post-tensioned slabs.

Environmental Engineering-I

101502 · 4 Credits
Unit 1.0 — Water 10 hrs
  • Sources of water and quality issues.
  • Water quality requirement for different beneficial uses, water quality standards, water quality indices and water safety plans.
  • Water supply systems; need for planned water supply schemes.
  • Water demand: industrial and agricultural water requirements.
  • Components of water supply system; transmission of water; distribution system.
  • Various valves used in W/S systems; service reservoirs and design.
Unit 2.0 — Water Treatment 5 hrs

Aeration, sedimentation, coagulation-flocculation, filtration, disinfection, and advanced treatments like adsorption, ion exchange and membrane processes.

Unit 3.0 — Air: Composition, Pollution and Monitoring 7 hrs
  • Composition and properties of air.
  • Quantification and monitoring of air pollutants.
  • Air pollution and occupational hazards.
  • Urban air pollution and automobile pollution.
  • Chemistry of combustion.
Unit 4.0 — Automobile Engines and Air Quality 7 hrs
  • Automobile engines, quality of fuel, operating conditions and interrelationship.
  • Air quality standards.
  • Control measures for air pollution, construction and limitations.
Unit 5.0 — Noise 5 hrs

Basic concept, measurement and various control methods.

Unit 6.0 — Building Plumbing 8 hrs
  • Introduction to various types of home plumbing systems for water supply and waste water disposal.
  • High-rise building plumbing.
  • Pressure reducing valves, break pressure tanks and storage tanks.
  • Building drainage for high-rise buildings.
  • Various kinds of fixtures and fittings used.

Engineering Economics, Estimation & Costing

101503 · 2 Credits
Unit 1.0 — Economics: Principles and Methodology 8 hrs
  • Basic principles and methodology of economics.
  • Demand/supply, elasticity, government policies and application.
  • Theory of the firm and market structure.
  • Basic macro-economic concepts: GDP, GNP, NI, disposable income and identities for closed and open economies.
  • Aggregate demand and supply (IS/LM); price indices (WPI/CPI); interest rates; direct and indirect taxes.
  • Public sector economics: welfare, externalities and labour market.
  • Monetary and financial system; central bank and monetary aggregates; commercial banks and their functions; capital and debt markets.
  • Monetary and fiscal policy tools and their impact on the economy — inflation and Phillips Curve.
Unit 2.0 — Business / Managerial Economics 7 hrs
  • Elements of business/managerial economics and forms of organizations.
  • Cost and cost control: techniques, types of costs, lifecycle costs, budgets and break-even analysis.
  • Capital budgeting; application of linear programming.
  • Investment analysis: NPV, ROI, IRR, payback period, depreciation and time value of money (present and future worth of cash flows).
  • Business forecasting — elementary techniques; cash flow and financial statements; case study method.
Unit 3.0 — Indian Economy 4 hrs
  • Brief overview of post-independence period and plans.
  • Post-reform growth and structure of productive activity.
  • Issues of inclusion: sectors, states/regions, groups of people (M/F), urbanization.
  • Employment — informal, organized, unorganized, public and private.
  • Challenges and policy debates in monetary, fiscal, social and external sectors.
Unit 4.0 — Estimation / Measurements 9 hrs
  • Introduction to estimation process; use of relevant Indian Standard Specifications.
  • Taking out quantities from given requirements of work; comparison of alternatives.
  • Bar bending schedules and mass haul diagrams.
  • Estimating earthwork and foundations; concrete and masonry; finishes, interiors and MEP works.
  • BIM and quantity take-offs; equipment costs; labour costs; rate analysis.
  • Material survey: thumb rules for material requirements for buildings, percentage breakup of cost, cost-sensitive index and market survey of basic materials.
  • Use of computers in quantity surveying.
Unit 5.0 — Specifications and Rate Analysis 5 hrs
  • Specifications: types, requirements and importance; detailed specifications for buildings, roads, minor bridges and industrial structures.
  • Rate analysis: purpose, importance and necessity; factors affecting it; task work; daily output from different equipment/productivity.
Unit 6.0 — Tender, Contracts and Bids 9 hrs
  • Preparation of tender documents; importance of inviting tenders; contract types and relative merits; prequalification.
  • General and special conditions; termination of contracts; extra work and changes; penalty and liquidated charges; settlement of disputes.
  • R.A. Bill and Final Bill; advance payment; insurance; claims; price variation.
  • Preparing bids and bid price buildup: material, labour, equipment costs, risks, direct and indirect overheads, profits.
  • Bid conditions, alternative specifications, alternative bids and bid process management.
  • Introduction to Acts pertaining to minimum wages, Workman's compensation, contracts, arbitration and easement rights.

Hydrology and Water Resources Engineering

101504 · 3 Credits
Unit 1.0 — Introduction and Precipitation 5 hrs
  • Introduction: hydrologic cycle, water-budget equation, history of hydrology, world water balance, applications in engineering and sources of data.
  • Precipitation: forms of precipitation; characteristics of precipitation in India; measurement of precipitation; rain gauge network; mean precipitation over an area.
  • Depth-area-duration relationships; maximum intensity/depth-duration-frequency relationship; Probable Maximum Precipitation (PMP); rainfall data in India.
Unit 2.0 — Abstractions from Precipitation 5 hrs
  • Evaporation process, evaporimeters, analytical methods of evaporation estimation, reservoir evaporation and methods for its reduction.
  • Evapotranspiration: measurement, equations, potential evapotranspiration over India and actual evapotranspiration.
  • Interception, depression storage and infiltration.
  • Infiltration capacity, measurement of infiltration, modelling infiltration capacity, classification of infiltration capacities and infiltration indices.
Unit 3.0 — Runoff 3 hrs
  • Runoff volume and SCS-CN method of estimating runoff volume.
  • Flow-duration curve and flow-mass curve.
  • Hydrograph, factors affecting runoff hydrograph, components of hydrograph, base-flow separation, effective rainfall and unit hydrograph.
  • Surface water resources of India and environmental flows.
Unit 4.0 — Groundwater, Well Hydrology and Water Uses 6 hrs
  • Forms of subsurface water, saturated formation, aquifer properties and geologic formations of aquifers.
  • Well hydraulics: steady-state flow in wells; equilibrium equations for confined and unconfined aquifers; aquifer tests.
  • Water for energy production, agriculture and hydroelectric generation; flood control; analysis of surface water supply.
  • Water requirement of crops and crop seasons in India; cropping pattern; duty and delta; quality of irrigation water.
  • Soil-water relationships, root-zone soil water, infiltration, consumptive use, irrigation requirement and frequency of irrigation.
  • Methods of applying water to fields: surface, sub-surface, sprinkler and trickle/drip irrigation.
Unit 5.0 — Distribution Systems 4 hrs
  • Canal systems, alignment of canals, canal losses and estimation of design discharge.
  • Design of channels: rigid boundary channels and alluvial channels.
  • Kennedy’s and Lacey’s theory of regime channels.
  • Canal outlets: non-modular, semi-modular and modular outlets.
  • Water logging: causes, effects and remedial measures; lining of canals and types of lining.
  • Drainage of irrigated lands: necessity and methods.
Unit 6.0 — Dams and Spillways 5 hrs
  • Embankment dams: classification, design considerations, estimation and control of seepage, slope protection.
  • Gravity dams: forces on gravity dams, causes of failure, stress analysis, elementary and practical profile.
  • Arch and buttress dams.
  • Spillways: components of spillways and types of gates for spillway crests.
  • Reservoirs: types, capacity, yield, regulation, sedimentation, economic height of dam and selection of suitable site.

Text / Reference

  2. K.N. Muthreja, Applied Hydrology, Tata McGraw Hill.
  3. K. Subramanya, Water Resources Engineering through Objective Questions, Tata McGraw Hill.
  4. G.L. Asawa, Irrigation Engineering, Wiley Eastern.
  5. L.W. Mays, Water Resources Engineering, Wiley.
  6. J.D. Zimmerman, Irrigation, John Wiley & Sons.
  7. C.S.P. Ojha, R. Berndtsson and P. Bhunya, Engineering Hydrology, Oxford.

Professional Practice, Law & Ethics

101505 · Audit / 0 Credits
Unit 1.0 — Professional Practice 8 hrs

Respective roles of various stakeholders:

  • Government: constituting regulatory bodies and standardization organizations, prescribing norms to ensure citizens' safety.
  • Standardization bodies (e.g. BIS, IRC): formulating standards of practice.
  • Professional bodies (e.g. Institution of Engineers (India), Indian Roads Congress, IIA/COA, ECI); local bodies/planning authorities: certifying professionals and offering platforms for interaction.
  • Clients/owners: role governed by contracts; developers: role governed by regulations such as RERA.
  • Consultants: role governed by bodies such as CEAI; contractors: role governed by contracts and regulatory Acts and Standards.
  • Manufacturers, vendors and service agencies: role governed by contracts and regulatory Acts and Standards.
Unit 2.0 — Professional Ethics 5 hrs
  • Definitions of ethics, professional ethics, business ethics, corporate ethics, engineering ethics and personal ethics.
  • Code of Ethics as defined on the Institution of Engineers (India) website.
  • Profession, professionalism, professional responsibility and professional ethics.
  • Conflict of interest; gift vs bribery; environmental breaches; negligence; deficiencies in state-of-the-art.
  • Vigil mechanism, whistleblowing and protected disclosures.
Unit 3.0 — General Principles of Contracts Management 9 hrs
  • Indian Contract Act, 1972 and amendments; general principles of contracting; contract formation and law; privity of contract.
  • Types of contract and features; valid and voidable contracts; prime and sub-contracts; joint ventures and consortium.
  • Contract terminology; tenders, requests for proposals, bids and proposals; bid evaluation; contract conditions and specifications; critical/red-flag conditions.
  • Contract award and Notice to Proceed; variations and changes; differing site conditions; cost escalation; delays, suspensions and terminations; time extensions and force majeure; delay analysis.
  • Liquidated damages and penalties; insurance and taxation; performance and excusable non-performance; contract documentation and notices.
  • Wrong practices in contracting (bid shopping, bid fixing, cartels); reverse auction; case studies; Build-Own-Operate and variations; Public-Private Partnerships; International Commercial Terms.
Unit 4.0 — Arbitration, Conciliation and ADR 7 hrs
  • Arbitration: meaning, scope and types; distinction between laws of 1940 and 1996; UNCITRAL model law; arbitration and expert determination; extent of judicial intervention; international commercial arbitration.
  • Arbitration agreements: essentials, kinds, validity, reference and interim measures by court.
  • Arbitral tribunal: appointment, challenge, jurisdiction, powers, grounds of challenge, procedure and court assistance.
  • Award: form and content, grounds for setting aside, enforcement, appeal and revision; enforcement of foreign awards — New York and Geneva Convention Awards.
  • Distinction between conciliation, negotiation, mediation and arbitration; confidentiality, judicial proceedings, costs; Dispute Resolution Boards and Lok Adalats.
Unit 5.0 — Labour and Construction-related Laws 5 hrs
  • Role of labour in Civil Engineering; methods of engaging labour — on rolls, labour sub-contract and piece-rate work.
  • Industrial Disputes Act, 1947; collective bargaining; Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946; Workmen’s Compensation Act, 1923.
  • Building & Other Construction Workers (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act (1996) and Rules (1998); RERA Act 2017; NBC 2017.
Unit 6.0 — Law Relating to Intellectual Property 8 hrs
  • Introduction and meaning of intellectual property; main forms of IP: copyright, trademarks, patents, designs and secrets.
  • Copyright law in India: historical evolution of Copyright Act, 1957; meaning of copyright including computer programs; ownership and assignment; infringement criteria; internet piracy, remedies and procedures in India.
  • Patents Act, 1970: concept and historical perspective of patent law in India; patentable inventions with special reference to biotechnology products; patent protection for computer programs.
  • Process of obtaining patent: application, examination, opposition and sealing; Patent Cooperation Treaty and grounds for opposition; rights and obligations of patentee; duration of patents; infringement and related remedies.

  2. The National Building Code, BIS, 2017; RERA Act, 2017.
  3. Meena Rao (2006), Fundamental Concepts in Law of Contract, 3rd edition.
  4. Neelima Chandiramani (2000), The Law of Contract: An Outline, 2nd edition.
  5. Avtarsingh (2002), Law of Contract, Eastern Book Co.; Dutt (1994), Indian Contract Act.
  6. Anson W.R. (1979), Law of Contract, Oxford University Press.

  8. Wadhera (2004), Intellectual Property Rights; T. Ramappa (2010), Intellectual Property Rights Law in India.
  9. Right to Information Act (2005); O.P. Malhotra, Law of Industrial Disputes.
  10. K.M. Desai, The Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act; Rustamji R.F., Introduction to the Law of Industrial Disputes.

Disability, Accessibility and Universal Design

101506 · Audit / 0 Credits
Unit 1.0 — Human Diversity and Inclusion 6 hrs

An introductory perspective: understanding concepts of diversity (including vulnerable groups), inclusion, need and significance, and impacts.

Unit 2.0 — Understanding Disability 5 hrs

Definitions, models and prevalence theory of disability; various concepts and models; prevalence.

Unit 3.0 — Disability Types and Environmental Needs 9 hrs
  • Disability classification, functional limitations and key coping strategies in the environment, for example physical and movement disabilities, and vision impairments.
  • Hearing, speech, cognitive, learning and other disabilities as per the RPWD Act 2016.
Unit 4.0 — Environmental Barriers, Codes and Policies 9 hrs
  • Introduction to and classification of physical, social and institutional barriers in diverse national and international contexts.
  • Introduction to Harmonized Guidelines, NBC and other exemplary international codes and guidelines; basics of accessibility codes and their review.
  • Legislative policies and programs: UNCRPD, RPWD Act, 2016, SDGs and urban development programs in Indian context; other international and national policies.
Unit 5.0 — Universal Design Theory I & II 8 hrs
  • Evolution from Barrier Free Environment to Universal Design.
  • Definitions, associated myths and concepts, terminologies and perspectives.
  • Universal Design principles (international, Indian, UD goals) and their criteria.
Unit 6.0 — Universal Design Case Studies 5 hrs

Built environment case studies from urban transportation and other contexts like peri-urban and rural settings.

Text / Reference

  1. Null, R. (2013), Universal Design: Principles and Models, CRC Press.
  2. Guffey, E. (Ed.) (2023), After Universal Design: The Disability Design Revolution, Bloomsbury Publishing.
  3. Lund, B. (2020), Creating Accessible Online Instruction Using Universal Design Principles, Bloomsbury Publishing.
  4. Burgstahler, S. (2020), Creating Inclusive Learning Opportunities in Higher Education: A Universal Design Toolkit, Harvard University Press.
  5. Cameron, C. (Ed.) (2013), Disability Studies: A Student's Guide, SAGE Publications.

Structural Design-I Lab

101501P · 1 Credit

Perform all experiments

  1. Design and drawing of singly reinforced, doubly reinforced rectangular and T-section simply supported and continuous beam.
  2. Design and drawing of one-way, two-way simply supported and continuous slab system.
  3. Design and drawing of dog-legged and open-well type staircases.
  4. Design and drawing of columns and foundation.
  5. Design and drawing of retaining wall (cantilever and counterfort).

Environmental Engineering-I Lab

101502P · 1 Credit

Perform all experiments

  1. Physical characterization of water: turbidity, electrical conductivity, pH.
  2. Analysis of solids content of water: dissolved, settleable, suspended, total, volatile, inorganic, etc.
  3. Alkalinity and acidity; hardness: total hardness, calcium and magnesium hardness.
  4. Analysis of ions: copper, chloride and sulfate.
  5. Optimum coagulant dose.

101503P · 1 Credit

Perform all experiments

  1. Deriving an approximate estimate for a multistoried building by approximate methods.
  2. Detailed estimate for the following with the required material survey: (a) Ground plus three storied RCC framed structure building with blockwork walls; (b) bridge with minimum 2 spans; (c) factory building; (d) road work; (e) cross drainage work; (f) ground plus three storied building with load-bearing walls; (g) cost of finishes and MEP works for (f) above.

  4. Assignments on rate analysis, specifications and simple estimates.
  5. Detailed estimate of minor structure.
  6. Preparation of bar bending schedule.
