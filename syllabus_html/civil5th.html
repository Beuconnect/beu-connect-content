Structural Design-I

Course code: Not specified in PDFL-T-P: 3-0-0Credits: 3
Unit 1.0

6 hrs

  • Fundamentals of RC Design and the Working Stress Method:
  • This unit introduces the basic material properties of concrete and steel, and the foundational
  • design concepts using the elastic theory.
  • It covers the traditional working stress method
  • (WSM) and its application to different types of beam sections.
  • Concepts of Reinforced Concrete: Introduction to RC, stress-strain characteristics of
  • concrete and steel, and the elastic theory.
  • Working Stress Method: Design philosophy and assumptions of the method.
  • Singly Reinforced Beam Analysis and Design: Analysis and design of balanced, under-
  • reinforced, and over-reinforced rectangular sections using the WSM.
Unit 2.0

9 hrs

  • Limit State Method: Principles and Design of Beams:
  • This unit transitions to the modern limit state method (LSM) of design.
  • It explains the core
  • concepts of LSM and applies them to the design of various beam types for flexure, shear, and
  • torsion.
  • Concepts of Limit State Method: Principles of probability and reliability, characteristic
  • loads and strengths, partial safety factors for loads and materials, and the different limit states
  • of collapse and serviceability.
  • Design of Beams for Flexure: Design of singly reinforced, doubly reinforced, rectangular,
  • and T-sections.
  • Design for Shear and Bond: Principles of shear resistance, design of shear reinforcement,
  • and ensuring proper bond and anchorage.
  • Design for Bending and Torsion: Design of beams subjected to combined bending and
  • torsion as per IS Code recommendations.
Unit 3.0

7 hrs

  • Design of Compression Members (Columns):
  • This unit focuses on the design of columns, which are critical elements for supporting axial
  • and bending loads.
  • It covers short and slender columns under various loading conditions.
  • Design for Axial Loads: Design of short columns subjected to direct compression.
  • Design for Uni-axial Bending: Design of columns subjected to combined axial load and uni-
  • axial bending moment as per IS Code methods.
  • Design for Bi-axial Bending: Design of columns for axial load and bi-axial bending
  • moment.
Unit 4.0

6 hrs

  • Design of Slabs and Staircases:
  • This unit covers the design of common slab systems and different types of staircases.
  • It
  • includes the design of one-way and two-way slabs, as well as complex slab systems like flat
  • slabs.
  • Design of Slabs: Design of one-way and two-way slabs.
  • Design of Flat Slabs: Design considerations for flat slabs.
  • Design of Staircases: Design of dog-legged and open-well type staircases.
Unit 5.0

6 hrs

  • Design of Foundations:
  • This unit addresses the design of various types of concrete foundations for transferring
  • building loads to the supporting soil.
  • Design of Isolated Footings: Design of square and rectangular isolated footings subjected to
  • axial load and moments.
  • Design of Combined Foundations: Design of combined footings for supporting multiple
  • columns.
Unit 6.0

8 hrs

  • Prestressed Concrete:
  • This unit introduces the advanced topic of prestressed concrete.
  • It covers the fundamental
  • principles, materials, prestressing methods, and the analysis of prestressed members.
  • Principles of Prestressed Concrete: Basic principles, materials, and methods of prestressing
  • (pre-tensioning and post-tensioning).
  • Losses in Prestress: Analysis of various types of losses, including elastic shortening, creep,
  • shrinkage, and friction.
  • Analysis of Prestressed Sections: Analysis of various types of sections (rectangular
  • T-
  • section, box section, and girders) subjected to prestress and external loads.
  • Design of Slabs: Design of post-tensioned slabs.

Water Quality and Management

Course code: Not specified in PDFL-T-P: 3-0-0Credits: 3
Unit 1.0

7 hrs

  • Introduction to water quality and management:
  • Fundamental concepts: Water resources and availability, the hydrological cycle, and global
  • water issues.
  • Water quality standards: Environmental legislation and regulatory standards for water and
  • wastewater.
  • Pollution sources: Classification of pollutants and the sources of water pollution,
  • including industrial, municipal, and non-point sources.
  • Necessity of management: The
  • importance of water management systems for public health and environmental protection.
Unit 2.0

7 hrs

  • Water quality analysis I:
  • Physical characteristics: Measurement and significance of physical parameters like solids
  • (TSS
  • TDS
  • VSS), turbidity, color, and temperature.
  • Chemical characteristics: Estimation and
  • analysis of key chemical parameters such as pH, alkalinity, acidity, dissolved oxygen (DO),
  • biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), chemical oxygen demand (COD), and total organic
  • carbon (TOC).
Unit 3.0

7 hrs

  • Water quality analysis II:
  • Biological characteristics: Waterborne diseases, microbial counts, and estimation of nitrogen
  • and phosphorus.
  • Sampling and testing: Protocols for sample collection, preservation, and
  • laboratory analysis.
Unit 4.0

7 hrs

  • Water treatment processes:
  • Conventional treatment: Detailed study of physical-chemical processes for drinking water
  • purification.
  • Screening and grit removal: Removal of large solids from raw water.
  • Coagulation and flocculation: Chemistry and mechanisms of adding coagulants to form flocs.
  • Sedimentation: Analysis of settling tanks for removing suspended solids.
  • Filtration: Principles, types (rapid and slow sand filters), and design of filtration systems.
  • Advanced treatment: Methods for removing specific contaminants.
  • Disinfection: Chemical
  • and non-chemical methods for killing pathogens.
  • Specialized processes: Techniques like
  • adsorption, reverse osmosis (RO), and ion exchange for removal of color, hardness, salinity,
  • and other contaminants
Unit 5.0

7 hrs

  • Wastewater treatment processes:
  • Introduction to wastewater: Sources, characteristics, and quantity estimation of sewage and
  • storm water.
  • Primary treatment: Screens, grit chambers, skimming tanks, and primary
  • sedimentation.
  • Secondary (Biological) treatment: Fundamentals of biological treatment
  • processes.
  • Suspended growth processes: Activated sludge process and its variations.
  • Attached
  • growth processes: Trickling filters and bio-towers.
  • Anaerobic processes: Advanced anaerobic
  • treatment methods.
  • Tertiary treatment: Emerging biological and advanced processes for
  • nutrient removal and final polishing.
Unit 6.0

7 hrs

  • Water resource management and quality control:
  • Self-purification: The natural processes of pollution decay in rivers and streams.
  • Reactor engineering: Application of mass balance and reaction kinetics to water and
  • wastewater treatment systems.
  • Case studies: Examples of treatment plant operations and
  • troubleshooting.
  • Integrated water resource management: An overview of holistic approaches
  • to managing water resources sustainably.

Course code: Not specified in PDFL-T-P: 3-0-0Credits: 3
Unit 1.0

7 hrs

  • Introduction to Offshore Geotechnical Engineering and Site Investigation:

  • Differences between onshore and

  • Offshore Site Investigation: Planning and execution of marine site investigations.

  • Sampling and In-situ Testing: Techniques for soil sampling in marine environments (box

7 hrs

  • Soil Behavior: Fundamental principles of soil mechanics applied to saturated marine soils.
  • Effective stress principle in submerged conditions.
  • Shear Strength and Consolidation: Shear strength of cohesive and cohesionless marine
  • soils.
  • Advanced consolidation theory and settlement analysis for offshore structures

7 hrs

  • Shallow Foundations for Offshore Structures:
  • Types of Shallow Foundations: Design considerations for gravity-based structures (GBS)
  • and mudmats.
  • Bearing Capacity: Calculation of bearing capacity under combined vertical, horizontal, and

  • Skirted Foundations: Design principles and installation of skirted foundations.
  • Settlement Analysis: Prediction of immediate and long-term settlement under cyclic and
  • sustained loading
Unit 4.0

7 hrs

  • Deep Foundations for Offshore Structures:
  • Pile Foundations: Design and installation of driven piles, suction piles/caissons, and drilled
  • shafts in offshore environments.
  • Axial and Lateral Loading: Analysis of piles under axial compression, tension, and lateral
  • loads.
  • Use of p-y, t-z, and Q-z curves.
  • Pile Group Analysis: Behavior and capacity of pile groups.
  • Suction Caissons: Principles of installation and capacity assessment for mooring and
  • foundation systems
Unit 5.0

7 hrs

  • Geotechnical Design for Pipelines and Anchors:
  • Offshore Pipelines: Geotechnical aspects of pipeline design, including on-bottom stability,
  • trenching, and backfilling.
  • Soil-Pipeline Interaction: Analysis of soil resistance to pipeline movement.
  • Anchoring Systems: Design of various anchor types (drag anchors, plate anchors, suction
  • anchors) used for mooring systems and floating platforms
Unit 6.0

7 hrs

  • Geohazards and Advanced Topics:
  • Marine Geohazards: Identification and mitigation of seabed instabilities, submarine

  • Cyclic Loading: Behavior of soils under wave and wind-induced cyclic loading.

  • pressure generation and liquefaction potential in marine soils.
  • Emerging Topics: Geotechnical challenges for offshore wind turbine foundations and other
  • renewable energy structures
