About the Examination

The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF) Examination is a national-level competitive exam for the recruitment of Assistant Commandants (AC), who are Group 'A' officers. This examination serves as the unified gateway for aspiring candidates to join the officer cadre of India's premier internal security forces. It is a highly respected career path that offers a unique blend of adventure, responsibility, and service to the nation.

📜 The Philosophy Behind the Examination

The CAPF (AC) examination is designed to select young, dynamic, and resilient leaders. Beyond academic knowledge, the selection process rigorously assesses a candidate's physical and mental fortitude, integrity, and suitability for command roles in challenging operational environments. The exam seeks individuals who possess:

  • Physical and Mental Robustness: The ability to endure tough physical conditions and maintain composure under extreme pressure.
  • Leadership and Command Quality: The potential to lead a company of armed personnel, make critical decisions, and inspire confidence.
  • Intellectual Acuity: A strong grasp of general awareness, analytical reasoning, and security-related issues.
  • Unwavering Integrity: A strong moral compass and a deep sense of patriotism and duty.

🛡️ The Forces at Stake

Successful candidates are appointed as Assistant Commandants in one of the five Central Armed Police Forces under the Ministry of Home Affairs. These forces are the pillars of India's internal security architecture.

Border Security Force (BSF)
Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF)
Central Industrial Security Force (CISF)
Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP)
Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB)

🪜 The Three-Stage Selection Process

The CAPF (AC) selection is a multi-stage process designed to comprehensively evaluate a candidate's fitness for the role. The entire cycle spans over a year.

  • Stage 1: Written Examination: This is the first filter and consists of two papers conducted on the same day. Paper I is an objective test of General Ability and Intelligence, while Paper II is a descriptive test of General Studies, Essay, and Comprehension. This stage assesses the academic and analytical capabilities of the candidates.
  • Stage 2: Physical Standards Test (PST) / Physical Efficiency Test (PET) & Medical Standards Test: Candidates who qualify the written exam are called for this stage. The PST involves checking physical measurements like height and chest. The PET includes a series of athletic events to test physical endurance and agility. This is followed by a detailed medical examination to ensure candidates meet the stringent health requirements. This stage is qualifying in nature.
  • Stage 3: Interview / Personality Test: The final stage is an interview conducted by a board of senior officers and experts at the UPSC. This test assesses the candidate's personality, leadership potential, communication skills, and overall suitability for a command position in the armed police forces.

🏆 The Final Verdict: Merit and Allocation

The final merit list is prepared based on the total marks obtained in the Written Examination (450 marks) and the Interview/Personality Test (150 marks), for a grand total of 600 marks. Based on their final rank and the preferences submitted by them, candidates are allocated to one of the five CAPFs.

Typical Timeline (Indicative)

The UPSC CAPF (AC) Examination follows a relatively predictable annual schedule. Understanding this timeline is essential for planning your preparation effectively.

⚠️ Disclaimer: These timelines are based on past trends. Always refer to the official UPSC calendar for the specific year for exact dates.

Phase 1: Notification & Application (April - May)

  • Late April: The official notification for the CAPF (AC) Examination is released by UPSC on its website (upsc.gov.in).
  • April - May: The online application window opens and typically remains active for about 20-25 days.

Phase 2: Written Examination (August)

  • July: The e-Admit Card for the Written Examination is released approximately 3 weeks before the exam date.
  • First Sunday of August: The Written Examination (Paper I & Paper II) is conducted on a single day across the country.

Phase 3: Written Exam Result (September - October)

  • The results of the Written Examination are typically declared within 40-50 days. A list of qualified candidates is published, who must then fill a Detailed Application Form (DAF).

Phase 4: PST / PET & Medical Tests (December - February)

  • Qualified candidates are called for the Physical and Medical tests. These are conducted in batches at various centers designated by the Nodal Authority (like ITBP, BSF, etc.).

Phase 5: Interview / Personality Test (May - June of next year)

  • Candidates who clear the physical and medical tests are summoned for the final interview at the UPSC office in New Delhi.

Phase 6: Final Result Declaration (July - August of next year)

  • The final merit list, based on the combined scores of the Written Examination and Interview, is declared, marking the end of the selection cycle.

Eligibility Criteria

The eligibility criteria for the CAPF (AC) exam are very strict, especially regarding age and physical standards. Candidates must verify their eligibility meticulously.

⚠️ Note: The official UPSC notification is the ultimate authority on all eligibility criteria.

🌍 1. Nationality

A candidate must be a citizen of India. (Subjects of Nepal and Bhutan are not eligible).

🎂 2. Age Limit

The age is calculated as of the 1st of August of the examination year.

  • Minimum Age:20 years.
  • Maximum Age:25 years. (A candidate must not have attained the age of 25 years).

Application Workflow

The application for the UPSC CAPF (ACs) exam is a completely online process conducted through the official UPSC website. It requires careful attention to detail to avoid rejection.

📝 Stage 1: One-Time Registration (OTR)

This is a mandatory, one-time process for all UPSC exams. Candidates must register on the OTR platform (upsconline.nic.in), providing basic details like name, date of birth, mobile number, and email ID. This information is saved and auto-populated for future applications, and it can be updated.

✍️ Stage 2: Filling the Examination Application Form

After completing OTR, candidates can log in and apply for the specific CAPF (ACs) Examination. This involves:

  • Part-I Registration: Confirming personal and educational details, and crucially, selecting preferences for the forces (e.g., BSF, CRPF, CISF, ITBP, SSB).
  • Part-II Registration: This involves paying the application fee, selecting the examination center, and uploading a recent photograph, signature, and a valid Photo ID document (like Aadhar Card, Voter ID, etc.) as per the specified format.

Application Fee

  • Amount: A non-refundable fee of ₹200 (Rupees Two Hundred only).
  • Exemptions: Female candidates and candidates belonging to Scheduled Caste (SC) and Scheduled Tribe (ST) categories are exempted from paying the fee.
  • Payment Modes: The fee can be paid online via Net Banking, UPI, or Credit/Debit cards, or offline by depositing cash at an SBI branch.

Key Points to Remember

  • Centre Selection: Exam centers are allotted on a "first-apply-first-allot" basis for the Written Exam. Apply early to get your preferred center.
  • Document Specifications: Ensure your photograph, signature, and Photo ID upload meet the exact size, format, and clarity requirements mentioned in the official notification to avoid rejection.

Syllabus

The syllabus for the CAPF (AC) exam is designed to test a candidate's broad-based knowledge and analytical skills.

📚 Paper I: General Ability and Intelligence (250 Marks)

  • General Mental Ability: Logical Reasoning, Quantitative Aptitude (including Numerical Ability and Data Interpretation).
  • General Science: General awareness, scientific temper, comprehension and appreciation of scientific phenomena of everyday observation including new areas of importance like Information Technology, Biotechnology, Environmental Science.
  • Current Events of National and International Importance: Knowledge of current events of national and international importance in the spheres of culture, music, arts, literature, sports, governance, societal and developmental issues, industry, business, globalisation, and interplay among nations.
  • Indian Polity and Economy: India’s political system and the Constitution of India, social systems and public administration, economic development in India, regional and international security issues and human rights including its indicators.
  • History of India: Social, economic and political aspects of the History of India. Also includes the areas of growth of nationalism and freedom movement.
  • Indian and World Geography: Physical, social and economic aspects of geography pertaining to India and the World.

✒️ Paper II: General Studies, Essay and Comprehension (200 Marks)

Part-A: Essay (80 Marks)

Candidates have to write descriptive essays on given topics. The topics are related to modern Indian history especially of the freedom struggle, geography, polity and economy, knowledge of security and human rights issues, and analytical ability. The essays can be written in either English or Hindi.

Part-B: Comprehension, Précis Writing, etc. (120 Marks)

This part consists of Comprehension passages, précis writing, developing counter arguments, report writing, and language/grammar based questions. This part must be attempted in English only.

Frequently Asked Questions

Here are answers to some common questions about the UPSC CAPF (AC) Examination.

❓ Regarding Eligibility & Application

  • Can I apply for the CAPF exam in the final year of my graduation?
    Yes. You are eligible to apply, but you must produce proof of passing your degree at the time of submitting the Detailed Application Form (DAF), which is after the written exam result.
  • Is there a limit on the number of attempts?
    No. There is no limit on the number of attempts, provided you fulfill the age criteria. You can appear for the exam as many times as you want between the ages of 20 and 25 (with relaxations for reserved categories).
  • Are there any relaxations in height for candidates from hilly regions?
    Yes. Relaxations in physical standards, including height, are available for candidates belonging to specified categories like Garhwalis, Kumaonis, Gorkhas, and those from certain North-Eastern states, as detailed in the official notification.

❓ Regarding the Examination

  • Is there negative marking in both papers?
    No. Negative marking of 1/3rd of the marks is applicable for incorrect answers in Paper I only. There is no negative marking in the descriptive Paper II.
  • Can I write the entire Paper II in Hindi?
    No. Only Part-A (Essay) of Paper II can be written in either English or Hindi. Part-B (Comprehension, Précis Writing, etc.) must be attempted in English only.
  • What happens if I fail the Physical Efficiency Test (PET)?
    Failure to qualify in any of the PET events will lead to immediate disqualification. Your candidature for that year's examination will be cancelled, and you will not proceed to the medical examination or interview stage.
